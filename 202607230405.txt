Alice : Hey, Bob! Are you ready for your big play tonight? I hope you don't break a leg! Bob : Alice! Wow, thanks! I hope I _don't_ literally break my leg, though. It's just a school play! Alice : Haha, of course not! "Break a leg " means "good luck"! It's a common phrase, especially for performers. Bob : Oh! That makes more sense. I was picturing myself in a cast. My lines, thankfully, were a piece of cake! Alice : A piece of cake? So, your lines were delicious? Bob : No, no! Another idiom! It means they were very easy to memorize. But this weather! It's raining cats and dogs out there! Alice : Raining *what* now? Cats and dogs? Are animals falling from the sky?! Bob : (Laughing) No! It means it's raining very, very heavily. Like a huge storm. My new raincoat, which must have cost an arm and a leg , isn't even helping much! Alice : "Cost an arm and a leg " means it was very expensive, right? That one I've heard before! Bob : Exactly! So, are you still going to the play despite the rain? Alice : Definitely! But I think we should hit the road soon if you don't want to be late. Bob : "Hit the road "? Like, hit the actual road with my hand? Alice : Haha, no, silly! It means "to leave" or "to start a journey"! Come on, let's grab an early dinner before the show! Bob : Okay, Alice! I'm learning so much today! Let's hit the road! Alice : Sounds like a plan!
