English Learning for Beginners: Phrasal Verbs

Dialogue

Alice: Hey Bob! What are you up to? You look like you're wrestling with a math problem.

Bob: Hi Alice! Worse. I'm trying to come up with a brilliant idea for our history project, but my brain feels empty.

Alice: Oh, don't put it off until the night before like last time! Remember we almost submitted a drawing of a potato?

Bob: (Laughs) Yes, and then we ran out of time to explain why it was "historically significant."

Alice: Exactly! Maybe we should look up some interesting facts about ancient civilizations?

Bob: That's a smart idea! I tried, but my laptop battery cut out just as I found a promising article. My luck!

Alice: Classic Bob. We could work at the library? They have outlets and caffeine.

Bob: Good call! But I actually just turned down Maria's offer to study together. She had a whole presentation planned.

Alice: You turned her down? Why? She's practically a walking encyclopedia!

Bob: I wanted to come up with something totally unique myself. You know, make my mark.

Alice: I admire the ambition, but sometimes teamwork helps figure things out faster.

Bob: You're right. I just really don't want to mess up this project. The last one was... memorable.

Alice: We won't! And hey, if we get stuck, we can always go over our notes again.

Bob: Thanks, Alice. You always know how to cheer me up when I'm stressing out.

Alice: Anytime! Now, let's get to it before the library closes!

Current Situation

English learners often find phrasal verbs a bit tricky, but they are super common in everyday English! A phrasal verb is a verb combined with a preposition (like "on," "off," "up," "down") or an adverb. The interesting part is that the meaning of the phrasal verb is often very different from the meaning of the original verb alone.

For example, "look" means to see something with your eyes. But "look up" means to search for information! These combinations make English sound very natural and fluent, so learning them is a great step to speaking like a native. Don't worry if they seem confusing at first; with practice, you'll start to recognize and use them easily.

Key Phrases

  • come up with: to think of an idea or a plan. Example: Can you come up with a new game for the party?
  • put off: to delay or postpone something. Example: Don't put off your homework until Sunday night.
  • look up: to find information in a book, on the internet, etc. Example: I need to look up the meaning of this word.
  • turn down: to refuse an offer, invitation, or request. Example: She had to turn down the job offer because it was too far.
  • run out of: to use all of something and have none left. Example: Oh no, we ran out of milk!
  • cheer up: to make someone feel happier. Example: Let's buy a present to cheer her up.

Grammar Points

1. What are Phrasal Verbs?

A phrasal verb is a verb plus a small word (like "up," "down," "off," "out"). Together, these two (or sometimes three) words act like a single verb with a new meaning. Think of them as special 'word teams'!

Example: go (to move) vs. go over (to review something).

2. How Phrasal Verbs Change Meaning

The trick with phrasal verbs is that you can't always guess their meaning from the individual words. You need to learn them as new words.

  • look means to see.
  • up means towards a higher place.
  • But look up means to search for information (e.g., in a dictionary or online).

3. Separable and Inseparable Phrasal Verbs (Simplified)

Some phrasal verbs can be "separated" by the object, and some cannot. This can be tricky, but here's a simple way to think about it:

  • Separable Phrasal Verbs: You can often put the object (the noun or pronoun) between the verb and the small word.
    • Example with turn down:
      ✓ I turned down the offer. (Object after)
      ✓ I turned the offer down. (Object in middle)
      ✓ I turned it down. (If the object is a pronoun like 'it', 'him', 'her', it usually goes in the middle.)
    • Example with cheer up:
      ✓ Let's cheer up Bob.
      ✓ Let's cheer Bob up.
      ✓ Let's cheer him up.
  • Inseparable Phrasal Verbs: The verb and the small word always stay together. The object always comes AFTER the phrasal verb.
    • Example with run out of:
      ✓ We ran out of milk.
      X We ran milk out of. (This is incorrect!)
    • Example with come up with:
      ✓ I need to come up with an idea.
      X I need to come an idea up with. (This is incorrect!)

Don't worry too much about remembering "separable" and "inseparable" for now. Just try to notice how they are used in sentences and practice with examples!

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1: Match the Phrasal Verb to its Meaning

Match the phrasal verbs from the list below to their correct meanings:

Phrasal Verbs: a) cheer up, b) put off, c) look up, d) run out of, e) come up with, f) turn down

  1. To refuse an offer or request.
  2. To find information (e.g., in a dictionary or online).
  3. To think of an idea or a plan.
  4. To use all of something, so there is none left.
  5. To delay or postpone doing something.
  6. To make someone feel happier.

Answers:

  1. f) turn down
  2. c) look up
  3. e) come up with
  4. d) run out of
  5. b) put off
  6. a) cheer up

Exercise 2: Fill in the Blanks

Complete the sentences using the correct phrasal verb from the key phrases section. (You may need to change the verb form, e.g., "ran out of" for past tense).

  1. My car broke down, so I had to __________ the meeting until next week.
  2. I need to __________ a good excuse for being late!
  3. We almost __________ sugar, so I bought some more.
  4. Sarah was sad, so her friends tried to __________ her __________ with a surprise party.
  5. If you don't know the word, you can __________ it __________ in the dictionary.
  6. He was offered a new job, but he __________ it __________.

Answers:

  1. My car broke down, so I had to put off the meeting until next week.
  2. I need to come up with a good excuse for being late!
  3. We almost ran out of sugar, so I bought some more.
  4. Sarah was sad, so her friends tried to cheer her up with a surprise party.
  5. If you don't know the word, you can look it up in the dictionary.
  6. He was offered a new job, but he turned it down.
