English for Beginners: Professional Speeches

Dialogue

Alice: Bob, I have to give a professional speech tomorrow! I'm so nervous!

Bob: Alice, you? Nervous? Just imagine everyone in their pajamas!

Alice: That doesn't help! Now I'm imagining my boss in a superhero onesie.

Bob: See? Fun! Just speak clearly. And don't forget to smile.

Alice: Smile? My face will probably freeze in terror! What if I forget my words?

Bob: Just make up new ones! "Ladies and gentlemen... uh... squirrels and acorns!"

Alice: Bob! This is serious! It's for the big project.

Bob: Okay, okay. Breathe deeply. And remember your opening line.

Alice: "Good morning, everyone." Simple, right? But my tongue feels like a pretzel.

Bob: Just pretend you're talking to me. I always listen... sometimes.

Alice: What if my slides don't work? Or I trip on the stage?

Bob: Then you get a standing ovation for your dramatic entrance! Just kidding.

Alice: Not funny! Any *real* tips?

Bob: Practice, practice, practice. And maybe wear comfortable shoes.

Alice: Comfortable shoes... good one.

Bob: And remember, even the best speakers get nervous. You'll be great, Alice!

Alice: Thanks, Bob. I hope so. Wish me luck!

Bob: Break a leg! (But not literally, especially with those comfortable shoes.)

Current Situation

In today's world, giving a professional speech or presentation is a very common skill, not just for managers or CEOs, but for almost everyone! From reporting on a project in an online meeting to presenting an idea to a small group, clear and confident speaking is essential. While it can feel scary at first, preparing well and understanding your audience can make a big difference. Many companies offer training, and there are countless resources online to help people improve their public speaking skills, making it a key area for personal and professional growth.

Key Phrases

  • I'm so nervous!
    Example: I have a test tomorrow, I'm so nervous!
  • Just imagine...
    Example: Just imagine a world without coffee! Terrible!
  • That doesn't help!
    Example: Telling me to calm down when I'm stressed? That doesn't help!
  • What if I forget my words?
    Example: What if I forget my words during the presentation?
  • This is serious!
    Example: Losing my passport is no joke; this is serious!
  • Breathe deeply.
    Example: When you feel stressed, try to breathe deeply.
  • Opening line
    Example: Her opening line for the speech was very engaging.
  • Pretend you're talking to me.
    Example: Don't be shy, just pretend you're talking to me.
  • Standing ovation
    Example: After his amazing performance, the audience gave him a standing ovation.
  • Just kidding.
    Example: I'm going to eat all your cake. Just kidding. (Mostly.)
  • Practice, practice, practice.
    Example: To get good at anything, it's practice, practice, practice.
  • Wish me luck!
    Example: I have a job interview tomorrow, wish me luck!
  • Break a leg!
    Example: Before you go on stage, I want to say, break a leg!

Grammar Points

1. "Have to" for Obligation

We use "have to" to talk about something that is necessary or an obligation. It means "must" or "it is necessary to."

  • I have to give a speech. (It is necessary for me to give a speech.)
  • She has to study for her exam. (It is necessary for her to study.)
  • We don't have to work on Sundays. (It is not necessary for us to work.)

2. "Will" for Future Predictions and Decisions

We use "will" to talk about things we expect to happen in the future (predictions) or decisions we make at the moment of speaking.

  • My face will probably freeze. (Alice's prediction about her face.)
  • You'll be great! (Bob's prediction for Alice.)
  • I will call you later. (A decision made now.)
  • It will rain tomorrow. (A prediction about the weather.)

3. Imperatives (Commands and Suggestions)

We use imperatives to give commands, make suggestions, or give instructions. We use the base form of the verb.

  • Just imagine everyone in their pajamas! (Suggestion)
  • Speak clearly. (Instruction/Suggestion)
  • Don't forget to smile. (Negative command/suggestion)
  • Breathe deeply. (Command/Suggestion)

4. "What if...?" for Hypothetical Situations

We use "What if...?" to ask about a possible future situation or consequence, often expressing worry or curiosity.

  • What if I forget my words? (Alice is worried about this possibility.)
  • What if my slides don't work? (Another worry.)
  • What if it rains tomorrow? We should take an umbrella.

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1: Fill in the Blanks (Key Phrases)

Choose the best key phrase from the dialogue to complete each sentence.

  1. I have a job interview tomorrow. Please, __________!
  2. My computer crashed! __________! All my work is gone!
  3. Don't worry about the audience, __________ you're talking to your best friend.
  4. I told him a funny joke, then I said, "__________."
  5. To master a new language, you need to __________.

Answers:

  1. Wish me luck!
  2. This is serious!
  3. pretend you're talking to me
  4. Just kidding.
  5. practice, practice, practice.

Exercise 2: Choose the Correct Grammar

Choose the correct word or phrase to complete the sentences.

  1. She (have to / has to) finish her report by Friday.
  2. (What if / Just imagine) we win the lottery?
  3. (Don't forget / Will forget) to turn off the lights.
  4. I think it (have to / will) be sunny tomorrow.
  5. You (have to / just imagine) study for the test.

Answers:

  1. has to
  2. What if
  3. Don't forget
  4. will
  5. have to

Exercise 3: True or False (Dialogue Comprehension)

Read the dialogue again and decide if the following statements are True or False.

  1. Alice is excited about her speech.
  2. Bob suggests imagining people in pajamas to help Alice.
  3. Alice thinks imagining her boss in a onesie is helpful.
  4. Bob tells Alice to make up new words if she forgets.
  5. Alice is worried about her slides and tripping.

Answers:

  1. False (She's nervous)
  2. True
  3. False (She says "That doesn't help!")
  4. True
  5. True
