English for Beginners: Understanding "Advanced Academic English"

Dialogue

Alice: Bob, guess what I saw today?

Bob: Alice, tell me! Was it a talking cat again?

Alice: Even weirder! A poster for a 'Symposium on Epistemological Frameworks in Post-Hegelian Dialectics'.

Bob: Whoa. That sounds... like a spell from a fantasy book. What even is 'epistemological'?

Alice: Exactly! It's what they call 'Advanced Academic English'. My brain cells are already sending their resignations.

Bob: Mine too! I think 'Hello, how are you?' is already advanced for me some mornings.

Alice: Right? I just want to order coffee without needing a PhD in Linguistics.

Bob: My dream is to understand a university lecture without falling asleep or consulting an oracle.

Alice: Maybe we should start a club: 'Beginners Against Big, Scary Words'!

Bob: I'd join! Our motto: 'Keep it simple, silly! And bring snacks!'

Alice: But seriously, sometimes I wonder how people learn all those mind-boggling words.

Bob: They probably download them directly into their brains. Or eat dictionaries for breakfast, with a side of thesaurus.

Alice: Haha! Or maybe they just study super, super hard. Like, with laser focus and special brain juice.

Bob: One day, Alice, one day we'll casually say 'interdisciplinary methodologies' and 'paradigm shifts' without even blinking.

Alice: And then we'll celebrate with simple, beginner-level ice cream. Extra sprinkles, please!

Current Situation

As English beginners, you're probably learning useful everyday phrases like "Hello," "How are you?", "Thank you," and how to ask for directions or order food. This is excellent! However, sometimes you might hear or see very long, complicated words, especially if you look at university websites, scientific articles, or formal reports. These words belong to what Alice and Bob humorously call "Advanced Academic English."

It's perfectly normal for these words to sound confusing or even funny to beginners. Academic English is a specialized form of the language, used in universities and research. It has its own vocabulary, longer sentences, and a very formal style. Don't worry if you don't understand it yet! It's a completely different level from what you are learning now, and it's something you will study much later if you pursue higher education in English. For now, focus on mastering basic, conversational English, and enjoy making fun of those "big, scary words" like Alice and Bob!

Key Phrases

  • 1. Guess what?

    Meaning: An informal way to say, "I have something interesting to tell you!"

    Example: "Guess what? I learned a new English word today!"

  • 2. Sounds like...

    Meaning: To describe how something appears or feels based on information you've heard.

    Example: "That new movie sounds like a lot of fun!"

  • 3. What even is...?

    Meaning: Expressing strong confusion or disbelief about something. Used informally.

    Example: "This recipe needs 'saffron threads'. What even is saffron?"

  • 4. My brain cells are sending their resignations.

    Meaning: A humorous way to say something is too difficult or confusing to understand, almost as if your brain is giving up.

    Example: "This math problem is so hard, my brain cells are sending their resignations!"

  • 5. Right?

    Meaning: Asking for agreement on something obvious or commonly felt.

    Example: "This coffee is delicious, right?"

  • 6. I just want to...

    Meaning: Expressing a simple, direct desire or need.

    Example: "I just want to relax after work."

  • 7. My dream is to...

    Meaning: Expressing a big wish or aspiration for the future.

    Example: "My dream is to travel the world one day."

  • 8. Keep it simple.

    Meaning: An idiom meaning to make something easy to understand or not complicated.

    Example: "When you explain the rules, please keep it simple for the beginners."

  • 9. Super hard / Super easy.

    Meaning: Informal way to say "very difficult" or "very easy."

    Example: "Learning a new language can be super hard, but also super rewarding."

  • 10. One day...

    Meaning: At some point in the future (often used for aspirations).

    Example: "One day, I will speak English fluently."

Grammar Points

1. Simple Present Tense (for facts, habits, and general truths)

The Simple Present tense is used for actions that are regular, true in general, or happen repeatedly.

  • Form: For most verbs, it's the base form (I eat, You play). For third-person singular (he/she/it), add -s or -es (He eats, She plays).

Examples from dialogue:

  • Alice: "It's what they call 'Advanced Academic English'." (call for 'they')
  • Bob: "I think 'Hello, how are you?' is already advanced for me..." (think for 'I', is for 'it')
  • Bob: "They probably eat dictionaries for breakfast." (eat for 'they')
  • Alice: "Sometimes I wonder how people learn all those mind-boggling words." (wonder for 'I', learn for 'people')

Remember:

  • I / You / We / They + verb (e.g., I like coffee.)
  • He / She / It + verb + -s/-es (e.g., He likes tea.)

2. Expressing Desires: "I want to + Verb" / "My dream is to + Verb"

These phrases are useful for talking about what you wish for or what you aim to do.

  • "I want to + [base form of verb]": This expresses a direct desire.
  • "My dream is to + [base form of verb]": This expresses a big, often long-term, aspiration.

Examples from dialogue:

  • Alice: "I just want to order coffee..."
  • Bob: "My dream is to understand a university lecture..."

More examples:

  • I want to learn English quickly.
  • My dream is to visit Canada.

3. "Without + -ing" (Expressing absence of an action)

We use "without" followed by the -ing form of a verb (a gerund) to show that something is done (or not done) in the absence of another action.

Form: without + verb-ing

Examples from dialogue:

  • Alice: "...without needing a PhD in Linguistics."
  • Bob: "...without falling asleep or consulting an oracle."
  • Bob: "...without even blinking."

More examples:

  • She left the house without saying goodbye.
  • You can't learn a language without practicing.

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1: Simple Present Tense - Fill in the blank with the correct form of the verb.

  1. Alice ______ (see) a funny poster.
  2. Bob ______ (think) academic words are hard.
  3. They ______ (joke) about complicated English.
  4. She ______ (want) to order coffee easily.
  5. I ______ (study) English every day.

Exercise 2: Expressing Desires - Complete the sentences using "I want to..." or "My dream is to..."

  1. (You) ______ learn more English words.
  2. (You) ______ speak English fluently one day.
  3. (You) ______ visit an English-speaking country.
  4. (You) ______ understand English movies.

Exercise 3: "Without + -ing" - Combine the two sentences into one using "without + -ing".

  1. I drink coffee. I don't add sugar.
    → I drink coffee ____________________.
  2. She passed the test. She didn't study much.
    → She passed the test ____________________.
  3. They watched the movie. They didn't make any noise.
    → They watched the movie ____________________.
  4. He finished his homework. He didn't ask for help.
    → He finished his homework ____________________.

Answers

Exercise 1:

  1. sees
  2. thinks
  3. joke
  4. wants
  5. study

Exercise 2: (Answers may vary slightly)

  1. I want to learn more English words.
  2. My dream is to speak English fluently one day.
  3. I want to visit an English-speaking country.
  4. My dream is to understand English movies. (or "I want to understand English movies.")

Exercise 3:

  1. I drink coffee without adding sugar.
  2. She passed the test without studying much.
  3. They watched the movie without making any noise.
  4. He finished his homework without asking for help.
